Personal Finance & Portfolio Management
Platform
Implementation Plan — Next.js + PostgreSQL

1. Project Overview & Assumptions
A single-user (extensible to multi-user) web app to track:
Income (multiple sources), daily expenses (cash/UPI/GPay/cards), and remaining balance
Investments (stocks, gold, silver, mutual funds, etc.) with live/manual % gain-loss
Net worth, computed automatically from all assets and liabilities
Credit score (CIBIL) history over time
Credit & debit cards, including credit card billing cycles (statement date, due date, outstanding)
Money lent to other people (loans given), how it was paid, and repayment tracking
A single dashboard summarizing all of the above in plain language
Assumptions made (easy to change):
1. Built first as a single-user personal finance tool — multi-tenant support is a Phase 7 add-on, not a rebuild.
2. "Civil score" = CIBIL score (India). The schema is bureau-agnostic, so Experian/Equifax/CRIF work the same way.
3. Credit bureau scores are entered manually/periodically (real-time bureau API access requires a business agreement — see Section 9).
4. Currency defaults to INR but every amount field is just NUMERIC, so multi-currency is a config change, not a schema change.

3. Feature Modules
1. Accounts & Cards — bank accounts, wallets (GPay etc.), cash-in-hand, credit/debit cards
2. Income — multiple income sources (salary, freelance, rental...) + individual income entries
3. Expenses — daily spend with payment method (cash/UPI/credit/debit), tagged to an account or card
4. Investments — stocks, gold, silver, mutual funds, etc., with purchase price vs. current price and % change
5. Credit Score — CIBIL/bureau score history with trend
6. Credit Cards & Billing — statement date, due date, outstanding, payment status per billing cycle
7. Loans Given — people who borrowed from the user, how the money was sent, repayment tracking
8. Net Worth Engine — periodic snapshot combining all assets minus liabilities
9. Dashboard — the single screen tying everything together

5. Application Architecture
App Router with route groups: (auth) for login/register, (dashboard) for the protected app.
Server Components for read-heavy pages (dashboard, reports); Server Actions for mutations (add expense, add investment) instead of hand-rolled API routes
where possible — less boilerplate, fully type-safe.
Route Handlers (/api/...) kept only for things that need a stable HTTP endpoint (e.g. a future mobile app, or webhook for price updates).
Prisma Client as a singleton (lib/db.ts) to avoid connection-pool exhaustion in dev (Next.js hot reload).
NextAuth with the Credentials provider initially; Google OAuth can be added without schema changes (NextAuth manages its own Account/Session tables
alongside users).

7. Dashboard Design
A single screen, top to bottom, in plain language:
1. Net Worth — large number + up/down arrow vs. last month
2. Credit Score Gauge — color-coded (red/amber/green) with last-updated date
3. Income This Month — total received, number of active income sources, and remaining balance (income − expenses so far)
4. Expense Breakdown — pie/bar chart by category and by payment method (cash vs UPI vs card)
5. Investments — total invested vs. current value, overall % gain/loss, broken down by stocks / gold / silver / other
6. Upcoming Credit Card Bills — card name, statement date, due date, amount due, days remaining
7. Loans Given — total amount out, who owes what, next expected return date, overdue flagged in red
Each card on the dashboard links through to its full module page for detail and editing.

8. Core Business Logic

Net worth formula (computed by a scheduled job into net_worth_snapshots):

Net Worth = (Cash & Bank Balances + Current Investment Value + Loans Receivable Outstanding)
− (Credit Card Outstanding + Other Liabilities)

Investment % change (per holding, and weighted-average for the whole portfolio):

% Change = ((Current Price − Purchase Price) / Purchase Price) × 100
Current Value = Quantity × Current Price

Credit card billing cycle: each card stores statement_day/due_day as defaults, but every actual cycle gets its own row in credit_card_bills (real banks shift
dates around month-end and holidays, so don't compute it blindly — record what the bank actually issued).

10. Security Considerations
Never store full card numbers — only last4.
Passwords hashed with bcrypt (handled by NextAuth Credentials provider).
All money tables scoped by user_id; enforce row-level checks in every Server Action/query, not just at the UI.
HTTPS everywhere; environment secrets via .env (never committed).
Rate-limit auth endpoints (e.g. Upstash Redis) to slow down brute-force attempts.
Consider an audit_log table later for who-changed-what, since this is financial data.
